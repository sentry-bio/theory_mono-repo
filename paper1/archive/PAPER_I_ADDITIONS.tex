\paragraph{The room's five names.}
The invariant this paper needs is $\hpack$ alone, and it carries five classical names, each under its own
hypothesis. In a complete Riemannian manifold of bounded geometry it is the \emph{volume entropy}
$\hvol=\lim_{\rho\to\infty}\rho^{-1}\log\mathrm{vol}\,B(o,\rho)$, the condition stated above. For a closed negatively
curved manifold, Manning's theorem identifies the volume entropy of the universal cover with the \emph{topological
entropy} of the geodesic flow \cite{manning1979}. For a discrete group of isometries of a proper geodesic space, orbit
growth gives the \emph{critical exponent} $\delta(\Gamma)$ of the Poincar\'e series, equal to $\hvol$ for cocompact
lattices \cite{patterson1976,sullivan1979} and, for geometrically finite Kleinian groups, to the \emph{Hausdorff
dimension of the limit set} \cite{bishopjones1997}. The \emph{Liouville entropy} of the geodesic flow is the fifth, and
it agrees with the others only in the locally symmetric case; in general it lies strictly below (Katok's inequality
\cite{katok1982}, with the rigidity theorem of Besson--Courtois--Gallot \cite{bcg1995}), and this paper never uses
it. The five coincide exactly where the host is as symmetric as Assumption~A3 of \S\ref{sec:chart} demands, and they
come apart outside it. Theorem~\ref{thm:bound} uses only $\hpack$, which exists in every metric space, so none of the
hypotheses behind the other four is a hypothesis of the bound; they are the names under which the same room has been
measured in dynamics, geometry and group theory. Read for heredity, they share one sentence: a lineage cannot branch
faster than its medium's geodesics diverge.

\begin{remark}[Four rigidities at the equality case]\label{rem:rigidity}
Equality in Theorem~\ref{thm:bound} is rigid in four independent senses; the Lean module \texttt{Rigidity} checks the
first and the others are classical.
\emph{(i) Net.} If $\log N(R)=\log P(B(o,r(R)),\eps)+o(R)$ along a sequence of radii, the address set is, at
exponential order, a maximal $\eps$-separated subset of the ball, hence an $\eps$-net of it
(\texttt{Rigidity.maximal\_separated\_isNet}); and a relational code of distortion $(D,K)$ whose book is an $\eps$-net
of the ball makes the source $(D,K+2\eps)$-quasi-isometric to the host (\texttt{Rigidity.quasiIsometric\_of\_net}). A
saturating genealogy is therefore not merely housed by its medium: at scale $\eps$ it \emph{is} its medium, and a
saturating tree forces a host that is a tree at scale $\eps$ (the min form of \S\ref{sec:IIb}).
\emph{(ii) Isotropy.} Under A3, Proposition~\ref{prop:schur} leaves one host, so the equality value $\bar\kappa^*$ is
unique.
\emph{(iii) Entropy rigidity.} Among metrics of fixed volume on a closed negatively curved manifold, the locally
symmetric metric alone minimizes volume entropy \cite{bcg1995}; a host that is also entropy-minimal is locally
symmetric.
\emph{(iv) Katok.} Liouville and topological entropy of the geodesic flow agree only for the locally symmetric metric
\cite{katok1982}; a saturating process whose dynamical and volume entropies agree is in that case.
None of the four is a claim about any biological system. Together they say that saturation is a \emph{design regime}:
reached by engineered codes, which can be laid down as nets, and approached, by the audit of Paper~II, by none of the
natural media measured there.
\end{remark}
