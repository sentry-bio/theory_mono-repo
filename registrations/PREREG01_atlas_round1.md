# Atlas test of the channel theory — pre-registration

Written 2026-09-25 before any held-out quartet accuracy was computed. Predictions are generated by
`atlas_predict.py` and saved (with timestamps) before `atlas_observe.py` computes observed accuracies.

## Setting

- Map: Biosphere Atlas base map v1.0 (recipe sha256:3b92fc2b…17fa), coordinates z (136-d) for 186,527 genomes.
- Depth: GTDB r220 bac120 tree; patristic distance τ in marker-protein substitutions per site.
- Genomes: catalogue genomes that are bac120 r220 leaves, one per leaf.
- Split: GTDB r220 orders assigned to train/test by md5(order) parity. Kernels and reader weights are fit on train
  orders only; all verdicts use test orders only.

## Layer attribution (already in hand, not re-tested)

The map's two DNA-disjoint halves test (alternating 100 kb blocks; nearest is own genome or species 98.9%) shows the
signal is genome-homogeneous. Inherited neutral record would not be shared between disjoint halves. The map therefore
reads the trait layer (heritable genome-wide settings), and the relevant law is the channel law with trait kernels,
not the neutral-substitution kernel. The neutral 4-mer horizon of about 0.2 substitutions per site is not tested here.

## Model (the theory, no free parameters at test time)

Genome coordinates are Gaussian with a tree-structured covariance: Cov(z_i, z_j) = S(τ_ij). Estimated from train
pairs as a variogram Γ(τ) = E[Δz Δzᵀ]/2 in τ bins, and Σ = Cov(z). Axes y = Uᵀz jointly diagonalise Σ and Γ(τ≈0.3);
each axis a then has a kernel c_a(τ) = 1 − γ_a(τ). A quartet's four-point outcome is predicted by Monte Carlo from
its six patristic distances and the per-axis kernels, for:
- the map's reader (Euclidean in z, i.e. metric G = (UᵀU)⁻¹ in y), and
- the optimal trait reader: Σ_a w_a Δy_a² with w_a = Δc_a / ((1 − c_a(2t+δ))² + Δc_a²) at the level's median (t, δ)
  from train quartets (the C3 optimal reader transferred to trait channels).

## Quartet levels (true topology from the tree)

L1 two genera of one family, two genomes each (the witness construction); L2 four genera of one family;
L3 four families of one order; L4 four orders of one class; L5 four classes of one phylum; L6 four phyla.

## Decision rules

D3 — channel law, pairwise → quartet (map reader), bins of t with ≥150 test quartets:
- PASS if |observed − predicted| ≤ 0.05 in every bin with predicted accuracy ≥ 0.6, and mean |error| over all bins ≤ 0.05.
- CONVERGENCE-LIMITED if the shallow bins pass but some bin with predicted accuracy < 0.6 has observed < predicted − 0.05.
- FAIL otherwise.

D4 — optimal trait reader, per level on test quartets:
- CONFIRMED if the gain over the map reader is ≥ +0.02 at L2 and L3, within ±0.03 of its predicted gain, and the
  loss at L1 is no worse than −0.01.
- REFUTED if the gain is ≤ 0 at both L2 and L3.
- Otherwise PARTIAL.

D5 — convergence past the horizon (direction pre-registered):
- Among map-reader errors, the share whose chosen pairing equals the GC-only pairing rises from L1 to L6
  (Spearman ρ > 0 over levels), and the per-bin shortfall (predicted − observed) is larger in quartets where GC
  groups genomes against the tree than in the rest.

## Amendment 1 — 2026-09-25 17:00, before any observed quartet accuracy

atlas_predict.py (run 16:59) found the per-axis approximation poor: after joint diagonalisation the off-diagonal
share of the variogram is median 0.40, max 1.89. Predictions that assume independent axes would test that
approximation, not the theory. Primary predictions are therefore replaced by the full-matrix Gaussian tree model:
Cov(z_i, z_j) = Σ − Γ(τ_ij) with matrix-valued Γ interpolated in log τ; the six quartet distances' means and
covariances follow exactly from trace formulas; accuracy = P(both wrong splits have larger sums) under a bivariate
normal approximation (validated against Monte Carlo on the per-axis model before use). The optimal reader per level
uses the basis that jointly diagonalises Γ(τ_sister) and Γ(τ_non-sister) at the train quartets' medians, with
weights w_a = (1 − μ_a)/(1 + (1 − μ_a)²), which is w = Δc/v in those units, clipped at 0. Decision rules D3–D5 are unchanged.
The per-axis Monte Carlo predictions stay saved as a secondary record.
